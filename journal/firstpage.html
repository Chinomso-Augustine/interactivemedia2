Form Design

Forms are everywhere on the web. Whether we are ordering items from Amazon or applying for jobs. Every aspect of a well-designed form serves the ultimate purpose of convincing a user to complete it with less stress. Here are five key decision-making principles in form creation that stand out to me:

  1. Minimizing Input Volume

    Minimizing the number of form fields is proven to decrease cognitive load and increase completion rates. By removing unnecessary questions, you reduce the overstimulation users often feel when faced with long and daunting forms.

  2. One-Column Layout

    Restricting forms to a single-column layout significantly increases completion rates because it maintains vertical momentum. A single column guides the eye straight down the page, while multiple columns interrupt the flow and confuse the user's visual journey.

  3. Multi-Step Organization

    Longer forms are intentionally organized into multiple steps to indicate progress and prevent burnout. Breaking information into easy chunks makes the overall task feel less overwhelming.

  4. Asking Easy Questions First

    Starting with simple questions keeps the momentum flowing. Simple questions encourage the user to keep going, whereas starting with difficult or sensitive questions can cause immediate friction and lead to incompleteness or delay.

  5. Explaining Input Formats

    Forms requiring passwords or specific input formats should include clear instructional text. Guiding the user through complex requirements reduces frustration

An example of a site I interacted with that embodies the majority of a thoughtful form is Handshake. Handshake’s sign up form is very minimal, asks simple questions first, explains input formats, and allows users to see their password before proceeding.
